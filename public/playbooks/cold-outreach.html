PRACTICAL PLAYBOOK · Two weeks

Cold outreach.

Start enough relevant conversations to learn which audience responds and why.

01

Define a narrow recipient: role, company type, problem, and why this person is relevant. Start with public Purdue alumni and program contacts, then expand to buyers outside Purdue.

MAKE THIS: A prospect description.
02

Build a 30-person research list. Record each person's public source, reason to contact, date sent, reply, follow-up, and outcome. Do not treat the alumni directory as consent for a bulk mailing list.

MAKE THIS: Source-backed outreach tracker.
03

Send a short personal note with one real reason for contacting that person and one small ask. Test two audience segments over 25–50 individually relevant messages; treat that volume as an experiment target, not a universal benchmark.

MAKE THIS: Sent messages and segment labels.
04

Follow up once after several business days with a useful observation or a clear close-out. Stop when asked. Record replies, booked calls, and confirmed problems rather than open rates.

MAKE THIS: Reply and conversation rates by segment.
05

After each conversation, ask who else has the problem and request an introduction only when there is a good fit.

MAKE THIS: Warm introductions and updated audience hypothesis.
Learning from a relevant alumnus
Subject: Purdue student researching [specific problem]

Hi [Name], I'm a Purdue [student/researcher/alum] speaking with [specific audience] about [problem]. I saw your work on [specific public project or role]. Could I ask you two questions in a 15-minute call next week? I'm trying to understand how people handle this today, not pitch a product. Thanks, [Name]
Offering a narrow paid pilot
Subject: Idea for [specific outcome] at [company]

Hi [Name], I noticed [specific public signal]. Teams like yours often spend [time or cost] on [problem]. I can help [specific result] in a [duration] pilot for [price], with [deliverable]. Worth a 15-minute look? If this is off target, no need to reply. [Name]
One respectful follow-up
Subject: Re: [original subject]

Hi [Name], one more thought: [useful observation or example relevant to their work]. If [problem] is on your list this quarter, I can share what I learned from similar teams. If not, I'll close the loop here. [Name]

What to watch out for

  • Generic mass mail to every listed alumnus
  • Inventing a connection
  • Automated follow-up after an opt-out
  • Treating volume alone as progress
